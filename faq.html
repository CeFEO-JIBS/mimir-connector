Questions

What it is, what it isn't, and how it handles your material.

What is Mímir?

A connector that gives Claude access to a curated library of family-business practice material. When you ask a question, Claude searches that library and answers from it, citing what it used.

How is this different from asking Claude directly?

Claude on its own answers from general training. Mímir answers from a specific, curated body of practitioner writing, research summaries and teaching material — and tells you which pieces it drew on, so you can check.

Does it show me the full articles?

No. Mímir reads the full text so it can reason accurately, but it gives you a synthesis and a link to the original on the publisher's own site. The source material stays with its publisher.

Who can use it?

Access is granted per email address. If you have been invited, sign in with that address.

What does it record?

Which tool ran, how many results came back and how long it took — enough to keep the service healthy and see what is useful. Not the content of your conversations with Claude.

Can it answer anything about family business?

It answers well where the library is strong: succession, ownership, governance, next generation, conflict, family offices, and the strategic questions around them. Ask about something outside that and it will tell you it found nothing rather than improvise.

Is it a substitute for an advisor?

No. It is a way to find what the field knows quickly and with sources. Judgement about a particular family stays with the people who know that family.

How often does the library grow?

New material is added as it is published. Items become searchable once they have been summarised and indexed.
